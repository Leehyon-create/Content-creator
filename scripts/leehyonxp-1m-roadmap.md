# LeehyonXP — The 1M Roadmap (YouTube + Instagram)

Target: 1,000,000 followers on YouTube and Instagram. Fully AI production.

This document is the arithmetic and the staged plan. It is deliberately unflattering
about the size of the number, because the number changes what you should build.

---

## 1. What 1M actually requires

Run the maths before choosing tactics.

A well-performing short-form account converts roughly **5 follows per 1,000 views**
(0.5%). Strong series content with a clear follow-trigger can reach 10–20 per 1,000;
generic viral content sits nearer 1–3.

At 5 per 1,000:

```
1,000,000 followers  ÷  0.005  =  200,000,000 views
```

Two hundred million views. Over three years at 5 posts/week — about **780 posts** —
that is an average of **~256,000 views per post**.

**That average is the whole strategy in one number.** Most posts will do 2–20k. So the
average can only be reached if a handful of videos do 5–20M each. Which means:

> 1M is not won by posting consistently good videos. It is won by building a format
> that can produce a 10M-view video, and then producing it repeatedly.

Consistency gets you to 10k. **Outliers get you to 1M.** Those are different skills,
and confusing them is the single most common reason accounts stall at 20k forever.

**Time horizon:** for accounts that make it, 1M typically takes **2–4 years**. Most
accounts never get there. Nothing below changes that base rate — it only maximises
your odds, which is the honest most any plan can offer.

---

## 2. Why "fully AI" makes this harder, and how to survive it

This is a business constraint, not a moral one, and it is worth knowing before you
build 780 posts on top of it.

Both platforms now discriminate against **mass-produced, repetitious content**.
YouTube's monetisation policy explicitly targets it. AI-generated content is *not*
banned — but low-effort, templated, indistinguishable AI output is exactly what those
policies were written to catch. A channel of generic AI anime edits is in the blast
radius. A channel with a consistent authored character is not.

**What keeps you on the right side of it:**

| Requirement | How you satisfy it |
|---|---|
| A consistent identity | Your trained Soul. Same face, every video. |
| Your own voice | Clone your voice once, narrate everything with it. |
| Original writing | The verdicts and the takes are yours. This is the real moat. |
| Non-templated output | Vary framing, environment and structure between episodes. |
| Disclosure | AI label on every upload, both platforms. |

The through-line: **AI is the camera, not the author.** Every video should be
identifiably yours in face, voice and opinion, even though no lens was involved. That
distinction is also what makes an AI channel scale past 100k instead of plateauing —
audiences follow a person, not a renderer.

## 3. The milestone ladder

Each stage is roughly 10x, and each requires a *different* thing. Trying to do stage-3
work at stage 1 is wasted effort.

### 0 → 1,000 · "does the format exist"
**Timeline: 1–3 months.** Post the numbered series on a schedule you can hold. Nothing
else matters. Do not change format, do not chase trends, do not read the follower count
daily. You are proving to yourself that you can produce 40 episodes without stopping.
**Exit test:** 40 episodes shipped, and one of them clearly beat the others.

### 1,000 → 10,000 · "find the first outlier"
**Timeline: 3–6 months.** Take the episode that outperformed and make three more like
it — same structure, same hook shape, different subject. You are hunting for what makes
*your* audience specifically react, which is never quite what you predicted.
**Exit test:** you can name why your best video worked, in one sentence, and you have
reproduced it at least twice.

### 10,000 → 100,000 · "repeatable outliers"
**Timeline: 6–18 months.** Now the series identity locks. Cold open, voice, verdict
format, and visual grammar stop changing. You add volume — 7+ posts/week, batched — and
start video-replying to your own comment section, which by now is generating material
faster than you can use it.
**Exit test:** you can predict, before posting, which of five videos will do best. And
be right most of the time.

### 100,000 → 1,000,000 · "scale and leverage"
**Timeline: 1–2 years.** Three things unlock here that were unavailable before:

1. **Collabs.** Other creators now say yes. This is the King Chris engine — roughly 40%
   of his distribution — and it finally becomes available to you. It is the single
   biggest accelerator in this stage.
2. **YouTube long-form.** See §4. This is where durable subscribers come from.
3. **A second format.** One series cannot carry a million people. Add a companion
   format — reactions, deep-dives, tier lists — that shares the audience.

## 4. YouTube and Instagram are not the same problem

You named these two specifically, and they behave very differently. Plan for both.

**YouTube — Shorts get views, long-form gets subscribers.**
A Shorts view is worth far less in subscriber terms than a long-form view. Channels
that hit 1M subs on Shorts alone exist but are the exception; the reliable route is
Shorts as the top of funnel and 8–15 minute videos as the conversion layer. Your
DaVinci timeline showed a 2:43 edit, so you can already cut long-form — that capability
becomes central around 100k.

**Suggested long-form format:** the same verdicts, at depth. *"Ranking every power
system in anime by how survivable it actually is"* is a 12-minute video that your entire
Shorts catalogue feeds into. Publish one per month from 50k onward.

**Instagram — slower per view, but stickier.**
Reels convert to follows at a lower rate than TikTok, so expect the follower count to
lag. Two things move the needle disproportionately: a **grid that reads as a portfolio**
(your AI cosplay stills are ideal — each cover frame a different character), and
**carousels**, which IG still favours and which cost you nothing since you're already
generating stills. Post the ranking as a carousel the same week the Reel goes out.

**Practical note:** keep TikTok running even though it's not in your goal. It's the
cheapest place to test which hooks work, and it costs one extra upload per post.

## 5. The next 90 days

Nothing in stage 0 requires the Soul, collabs, or a budget.

- **Ship 40 episodes.** Non-negotiable. This is the only stage-0 task.
- **Lock the five constants:** cold open card, VO voice, verdict format, closing line,
  caption style. Changing these restarts your recognisability from zero.
- **Clone your voice once**, then narrate everything with it. Do this early — voice
  consistency across 780 posts is what makes the catalogue feel authored.
- **20 minutes a day** in the comment sections of large accounts in your lane.
- **Review at day 90**, then change exactly one variable.

**Do not** buy followers, do not run engagement pods, do not post to Reels with a
TikTok watermark, and do not restart the account because early numbers are low. Those
four things kill more accounts at this stage than bad content does.

---

## 6. The honest summary

1M on two platforms is a **2–4 year project**, most attempts fail, and the outcome is
decided by whether you can produce repeatable outliers rather than by whether you post
consistently — though you must post consistently to get the chance.

Fully-AI production makes it harder, not easier, because it puts you in a category the
platforms are actively filtering. The counter is authorship: your face, your voice,
your takes, every time.

The good news is that stage 0 is entirely within your control, needs nothing you don't
already have, and is the same 40 episodes regardless of which stage-3 tactics turn out
to matter. So the plan for the next three months is not in doubt — only the execution is.
