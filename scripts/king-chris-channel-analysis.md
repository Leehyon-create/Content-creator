network egress proxy, so no video was watched frame-by-frame. This breakdown is

timings* as an informed template to test, not measured data.

## 2. Content pillars

Sorted by how often they show up in the catalogue:

**P1 — Cosplay character skit (the flagship).**
He plays multiple anime characters in one clip via wardrobe/wig swaps and match-cut
transitions. Examples: *"who wore it better?🐰 @itsKingChris #yorforger"*,
*"I'll teach you everything 💜 #chainsawman"*, Spy x Family / Anya bits.

**P2 — Fan-archetype comedy ("mfs who…").**
Observational skits about anime-fan behaviour. Example: *"Mfs that watch obscure
Anime😭"*. Zero production cost, highest comment volume, most quotable.

**P3 — Game / challenge series.**
Numbered, franchisable, built for Part 2. Examples: *"Anime Penalty Match with
@itsKingChris"* → *"Anime Penalty Part2"*, *"I Did Not Expect This Pt.9"*.

**P4 — Reaction + tier list.**
Ranking and hot-take formats inherited from the long-form era. Comment-bait by design.

**P5 — Collab / duet bait.**
`@TheAnimeMen`, `@brownbakugo`, `@giaisabellag`, `@Jose Estrada`. He appears *in the
title* of other creators' uploads, which is distribution, not vanity.

## 3. Hook taxonomy

Every title in the catalogue maps to one of six hook mechanics. This is the most
copyable thing about the channel.

| # | Hook type | Live example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **Deferred payoff** | `'WAIT FOR END'💀` | Explicitly buys retention. Costs one word. The 💀 promises the payoff is funny, not sincere. |
| H2 | **Comparison / verdict** | `who wore it better?🐰` | Forces a comment. Binary questions get answered by people who'd never write a sentence. |
| H3 | **In-group callout** | `Mfs that watch obscure Anime😭` | Viewer self-identifies in frame 1. "That's literally me" is the highest-value comment in the niche. |
| H4 | **Serialised number** | `Pt.9`, `Part2` | Signals a proven format; the number itself is social proof. |
| H5 | **Broken-expectation** | `I Did Not Expect This` | Curiosity gap with no subject noun. Reader has to watch to resolve it. |
| H6 | **One-word question** | `Arise?` | Franchise trigger word (Solo Leveling). Fandom decodes it instantly; outsiders scroll. Intentional filtering. |

**Pattern across all six:** the hook lives in the *title/caption + first frame
simultaneously*. Nothing is held back for a "welcome back to the channel." There is
no intro.

## 4. Format anatomy (the reusable skeleton)

```
0.0–1.5s   COLD OPEN. Character already on screen, mid-action, in costume.
           Big caption locks the premise. No logo, no greeting, no "so basically."
1.5–8s     SETUP. Establish the archetype or the rule of the game.
8–22s      ESCALATION. 3 beats, each a harder version of the last.
           One hard cut per beat — roughly one cut every 1.5–2.5s.
22–32s     TURN. The thing the title promised. Reverse the power dynamic.
32–38s     BUTTON + LOOP. Final line lands on a beat, then cut to black
           mid-syllable so the loop restart feels intentional.
```

**Editing signature** (consistent with his AE/Premiere tutorial background):

- Whip-pan / match-cut on wardrobe swaps — never a dissolve.
- Speed ramps into punchlines, freeze-frame on reaction faces.
- Anime SFX stingers (JJK domain-expansion whoosh, DBZ power-up, sitcom "bruh" bass drop).
- Captions burned in, centre-frame, 3–6 words max, one line at a time. Muted-autoplay-proof.
- Trending audio underneath, ducked ~10dB under dialogue.

**Structural rules worth stealing:**

1. Costume change *is* the transition. Never cut away to change — cut *on* the change.
2. Every clip is duet-shaped: leave a beat of dead air at the end where someone else replies.
3. Series-number anything that performs. Pt.2 inherits Pt.1's audience.
4. One joke per video. Escalation is not a second joke, it's the same joke louder.
5. Payoff must invalidate the setup — the last 4 seconds should make the first 4 mean something different.

---

## 5. What to copy vs. what to skip

**Copy:** cold open, burned-in captions, one-joke discipline, cut-on-costume-change,
serialised numbering, collab tags in the title, the loop-friendly ending.

**Skip:** don't imitate his subscriber-dependent moves — the Q&A episodes, the "ft."
collabs with big accounts, and the tier-list formats all assume an audience that
already argues in your comments. Start on P2 (fan-archetype comedy): it needs one
person, one room, one costume, and it's the pillar where a small channel can beat a
big one on writing alone.
