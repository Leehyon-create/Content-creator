# LeehyonXP — Growth Plan (starting from a small base)

**Assumptions this is built on** (correct me and I'll rework it):
- Faceless production — AI-generated anime visuals, game footage, burned-in captions, TTS or your own voice.
- Currently: TikTok `@leehyonxp` posts AI anime; YouTube `LeehyonXP` is branded "XP Family," a general gaming channel.
- Target: TikTok + Instagram Reels + YouTube Shorts, simultaneously.
- Cadence: designed for 5–7 posts/week produced in **one batching session**. It degrades gracefully to 3–4.

---

## 1. The honest diagnosis

Three problems, in order of how much they're costing you.

**Problem 1 — the brand is split.** Your TikTok is an anime account. Your YouTube is a
gaming account called XP Family. Those feed two different algorithms two different
signals, and neither builds on the other. A viewer who likes the Gojo post has no
reason to subscribe on YouTube, because YouTube is showing them something else. From
a small base you cannot afford to run two positioning experiments at once.

**Problem 2 — AI anime edits are a commodity.** There are thousands of accounts posting
AI-generated Gojo. The visuals are no longer a differentiator, because anyone can
produce them in ninety seconds. When the supply of a format goes infinite, the only
scarce thing left is **the take** — the writing, the opinion, the verdict. That is where
your effort has to move. Not better renders. Better arguments.

**Problem 3 — you are optimising for views when you need follows.** These are different
mechanics and this is the single most important thing in this document. A video gets
*views* because the first two seconds stop the scroll. A video gets a *follow* because
the viewer believes **the next one will also be good**. Most small creators go viral once,
gain 400 followers off 800k views, and conclude the algorithm hates them. What actually
happened is nothing in the video promised a next one.

Everything below is designed around problem 3.

---

## 2. Positioning: the lane

**Own "anime power systems and progression, judged."**

Not "anime." Not "gaming." The overlap that your own brand name already points at:
**XP, levelling, power scaling, progression, who-beats-who**. It is the single most
argued-about topic in the anime fandom, it maps directly onto game mechanics, and it
justifies both halves of what you're already posting without abandoning either.

This gets you:
- A reason for the anime audience and the gaming audience to be the same audience.
- Infinite content supply — every new season and every new game restocks it.
- Built-in comment conflict. Power scaling arguments are the most reliable comment
  engine in the niche, and comments are distribution.
- A name that finally means something. **XP Family** stops being generic and starts
  being literal.

**One-line bio, all three platforms:**
> LeehyonXP — power systems, ranked. Would you survive?

Same handle, same avatar, same bio, same pinned video everywhere. A viewer who finds
you on TikTok must be able to recognise you instantly on YouTube. Right now they can't.

## 3. The follow-trigger: one numbered flagship series

From zero, you do **not** post seven unrelated videos a week. You post one **series**,
numbered, with an identical cold open every time, until the format is recognisable
within half a second.

**Flagship: "WOULD YOU SURVIVE?" — Ep. 1, 2, 3, …**

One anime power system per episode, explained fast, then a verdict on whether an
ordinary person could survive it. Ends on a direct question to camera.

Why this specific format works from a small base:

| Property | Effect |
|---|---|
| Numbered episodes | The number is social proof. Ep.14 looks like a channel that delivers. |
| Identical cold open | Builds recognition faster than any logo. Returning viewers self-identify in 0.5s. |
| Ends on a binary question | Binary questions get answered by people who never comment. |
| Verdict format | Someone will always disagree with the verdict. Disagreement is free reach. |
| Infinitely restockable | Every airing season hands you ten more episodes. |
| Faceless-native | Needs AI visuals, captions, and a voice. No performance, no set, no wardrobe. |

**The follow-trigger is the number.** Everything else is craft; the number is the
mechanism. It's the same lever King Chris uses with "Pt.9" and "Part2" — see the
hook taxonomy in `king-chris-channel-analysis.md` — and it is the *one* part of his
playbook that works identically at 500 followers and at 20 million, because it
doesn't depend on an audience already existing.

**Ratio:** 4 flagship episodes + 2–3 supporting posts per week. The supporting posts
chase trends and test hooks; the flagship builds the following.

## 4. What of King Chris's playbook you can and cannot use

His engine has a component you don't have yet. Be clear-eyed about which is which.

| His lever | Available to you? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open, no intro | ✅ Yes | Use it. Non-negotiable. |
| Burned-in captions | ✅ Yes | Use it. Every video plays muted first. |
| One joke / one idea per video | ✅ Yes | Use it. This is discipline, not budget. |
| Serialised numbering | ✅ Yes | **Your highest-leverage move.** |
| Payoff that invalidates the setup | ✅ Yes | Use it — your verdict is the payoff. |
| Loop-friendly hard cut ending | ✅ Yes | Use it. |
| Collabs / duet tags with big accounts | ❌ Not yet | See §5 for the replacement. |
| Comment-section tier list wars | ⚠️ Partly | Works only once you have commenters. The verdict format seeds them. |
| Q&A / audience-request content | ❌ Not yet | Needs an audience to request. Revisit at ~10k. |

**The gap:** roughly 40% of his distribution comes from other creators tagging him.
You cannot buy into that at your size. §5 is what replaces it.

## 5. Distribution without collabs

Four substitutes, in order of return on effort.

**5.1 — Reply-farming (the strongest one).**
Find the top 20 comments on *large* accounts in your exact niche, posted within the
last 2 hours, and leave a genuinely good take — not "great video," an actual opinion
someone would screenshot. Your profile gets clicked from comment sections far more
than most small creators realise. Budget 20 minutes a day. This is the closest free
substitute for being tagged by a big account.

**5.2 — Video replies to comments.**
The moment anyone disagrees with a verdict, make a video reply. It's a native format
on all three platforms, it starts you with an argument already in progress, and it
converts one commenter into a returning viewer. This is how the flagship series
compounds without you doing anything extra.

**5.3 — Trend-jacking, but only in your lane.**
When a season drops or a game patches, post within 24 hours. Not because trends are
magic, but because search and hashtag traffic for a brand-new show has almost no
competing supply on day one. This is the one window where a small account
out-competes a large one.

**5.4 — Cross-posting mechanics (do this properly, it's free reach).**
- Export **clean** from your editor. Never save from the TikTok app and re-upload —
  the watermark is downranked on Reels and Shorts.
- Same video, three captions. TikTok: conversational, ends in the question. Instagram:
  front-load searchable keywords, 3–5 hashtags. YouTube: a real title with the anime's
  name in it, because Shorts search behaves like YouTube search.
- Stagger by a few hours; don't post to all three at the same minute.
- Instagram Reels needs a **cover frame** with legible text, because your profile grid
  is a landing page. TikTok and Shorts don't care nearly as much.

## 6. Batching: one session, one week of content

Do not produce daily. Daily production is how people quit in month two.

**One session, ~4 hours, produces 7 posts:**

1. **Write all 7 first** (60 min). Scripts only. No visuals yet. Writing in a batch keeps
   the voice consistent and makes weak concepts obvious next to strong ones.
2. **Generate all visuals** (90 min). Same visual grammar across all 7 — same font,
   same caption position, same transition set, same colour treatment. Consistency is
   what makes a small account look established.
3. **Record all VO in one take-run** (30 min). Same mic position, same energy, same room.
4. **Assemble from a template project** (60 min). Build the project file once, then only
   swap the media. Your edit should be a fill-in-the-blank, not a fresh build.
5. **Export clean + write 21 captions** (20 min). 7 videos × 3 platforms.

Schedule them out across the week. Then spend your daily 20 minutes on §5.1 — that's
your only daily obligation.

## 7. What to actually measure

Ignore follower count for the first 60 days. It's the laggiest metric you have and
watching it daily will make you change strategy too often — which is the actual
failure mode.

| Metric | Where | What good looks like | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retention at 3s** | All three | >70% | Is your hook working? Fix this before anything else. |
| **Average % watched** | All three | >55% on a 30s video | Is your middle sagging? |
| **Comments per 1k views** | All three | >4 | Is the verdict provoking a response? |
| **Follows per 1k views** | TikTok/IG | >2 | **The number that actually matters.** |
| **Returning-viewer %** | YouTube Studio | rising, any rate | Is the series working as a series? |

**The diagnostic:** high views + low follows means the video was entertaining but
promised nothing. Strengthen the series signal — the number, the cold open, the
outro. High follows + low views means the concept is strong but the hook is weak.
Reshoot the first two seconds and repost it in three weeks; almost nobody saw it.

## 8. First 30 days

**Week 1 — unify.** Same handle, avatar, bio, and pinned post on all three. Rebrand the
YouTube from generic "XP Family" gaming to the power-systems lane. Post Ep.1–4 of the
flagship. Start the daily 20-minute comment routine.

**Week 2 — read the data.** Post Ep.5–8. Take your best-retaining hook from week 1 and
reuse its structure twice. Reply-video to every disagreement you get, however small.

**Week 3 — push the winner.** By now one episode will clearly outperform. Make the
sequel to *that specific one*, and put its episode number in the title. Post the
follow-up within 72 hours while the original is still being served.

**Week 4 — consolidate.** Post Ep.13–16. Repost your two best week-1 videos with
rebuilt first-two-seconds — under 5% of your eventual audience saw them, so they are
not "used up." Review the table in §7 and change exactly **one** variable for month 2.

**One variable at a time.** The most common way small channels stall is changing
format, hook, topic, and posting time all in the same week, then having no idea what
worked.

---

## 9. The part nobody says out loud

At a small size, most of your videos will do nothing. That's not a signal — it's the
base rate. Short-form growth is not linear; it's flat, flat, flat, then one video
opens the door and the *back catalogue* converts the traffic into followers. Which
means the catalogue has to exist and has to be recognisably one thing when that
happens.

So the actual job for 90 days is: post the same recognisable series, numbered, on a
schedule you can hold, and be findable and consistent when your moment arrives. The
plan above is entirely in service of being ready for it.
